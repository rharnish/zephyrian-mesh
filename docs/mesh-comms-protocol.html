Mesh Comms Protocol

How a balloon's telemetry actually gets to the ground: discovery by beacon flood, store-and-forward bundles along a locally-believed route, a source-routed tower acknowledgement that can legitimately go missing, and satellite as the fallback of last resort. Nothing here is computed for a balloon by the server — every belief is built only from signals that physically arrived.

This page diagrams one configuration: dv-dtn with default parameters — proactive beacon discovery and source-routed acks. That is what ships, but it is one of several. Discovery can instead be reactive (AODV-style) or gossiped link-state via discovery=; acks can ride tower beacons instead of travelling as packets via ack=digest; and epidemic replaces routing altogether with binary spray-and-wait. See docs/design/PROTOCOL_REFERENCE.md for all of them and the measured differences between them.

Discovery beacon flood → route belief

Towers periodically emit a beacon. Any balloon that hears one records a RouteBelief (tower, hop count, which neighbor it heard it from) and rebroadcasts one hop further — but only if the belief actually improved, which is what keeps a flood from becoming a storm. A belief's age is measured from when the tower emitted it, never refreshed by a relay repeating stale news — the opposite rule lets a cut-off cluster sustain dead routes forever.

Bundle lifecycle store-and-forward along a believed route

A balloon with telemetry to send forwards to whichever neighbor its belief currently names — a local, possibly stale, possibly suboptimal choice, never a recomputed shortest path. Each relay appends its own id, which buys loop detection, a hop budget, and provenance in one field. A stale next hop makes the bundle wait, not vanish — that's what makes this delay-tolerant.

Tower ack & satellite fallback the receipt can get lost too

On arrival, the tower's ack is source-routed back along the bundle's own recorded path, reversed — one hop per wake slot, and it wins contention over ordinary bundle forwarding at a relay so it isn't quietly starved. The reverse path can break after the forward trip already succeeded, which is a real, load-bearing failure mode, not an edge case to paper over.

From the balloon's own side, "my data never arrived" and "it arrived and the receipt died" are the same observation — TimedOut covers both, plus the case where satellite delivered it silently. That's the whole point of the two-state model:

Parameters that set the pacing

Rounds, not ticks — the comms protocol runs on its own clock so its real-time pace is tunable independent of physics smoothness.

These are DvDtnParams fields (sim-server/src/protocol/dv_dtn/params.rs), not global constants: the values below are the Default impl, and most are overridable per run through the --protocol spec — see ./run-all.sh --help.

ParameterDefaultGoverns
beacon_interval_rounds5Duty-cycle gap between transmissions — also the bundle/ack forwarding rate
belief_max_age_rounds60How long a route belief survives, keyed on the tower's emission round
bundle_interval_rounds200How often a balloon originates new telemetry
bundle_max_age_rounds150Satellite-fallback timeout — doubles as the ack round-trip budget
bundle_max_hops20Hop budget before a bundle is dropped as TTL-expired
relay_queue_capacity8Bundles a relay may hold at once
ack_queue_capacity4Acks a relay may hold at once — much lower volume than bundles
batch.tower_contact4Bundles drained per tower contact — the last-hop's one real lever

What's actually measured

Swept with the real protocol running (bin/protocol_sweep.rs, real wind, 15 cells across horizon coefficient × balloon count, 10 seeds each) rather than the retired toy accounting model. Ground truth and belief both saturate just past the percolation threshold, as expected — but delivery lags behind at every density, and the round trip fares worst: above the threshold acks are lost for more bundles than they come home for, and even in the densest mesh no more than ~36% of bundles are delivered and acknowledged.
